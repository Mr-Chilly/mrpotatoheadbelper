SHE SPEAKS.

It was 9:47 on a wet Friday night in Belper. The kind of night where the rain doesn't fall — it interrogates you. Week twelve of the "repairs". Not one photograph. Not one phone call. Then the press-room doors swung open, the flashbulbs went off like a fryer catching, and a two-metre wife sat down in front of the microphones. She had pearl earrings, a steady voice, and four words the establishment never wanted broadcast: "I want him back."

The Statement · Read Into The Record

"I Will Find You. And I Will Mash You."

Delivered without notes. Without a lawyer. Without blinking — although, in fairness, her eyes clip on. Reproduced here in full, because the BBC certainly won't.

I don't know who you are. I don't know what you want. If you are looking for ransom, I can tell you we don't have money. We spent it on novelty ears.

But what I do have are a very particular set of skills. Skills I have acquired over twenty-five years of marriage to a two-metre potato. Skills that make me a nightmare for people like you.

If you return my husband now — both arms, big daft grin, absolutely no seasoning — that'll be the end of it. I will not look for you. I will not pursue you.

But if you don't… I will look for you. I will find you. And I will mash you.

— Mrs Potato Head · Belper P.D. press room · 21:47

At this point the council's press officer is reported to have said "good luck" down a phone line, after which the line went quiet for some time. A kettle in a council depot is understood to have gone cold. The Daily Peel is not in a position to comment further.

The Man On The Case

Three Days From Retirement.
Twelve Minutes From Perfect.

Belper P.D. has assigned its most decorated, least stable officer. He doesn't play by the book. He owns one book — Delia Smith's "How To Cook, Book One" — and he has never got past the eggs chapter. He says everything you need to know about this town is in the eggs chapter.

D.I. Terry "Sunny Side" Upton

Detective Inspector · Spud Squad (est. Tuesday)

Status
Three days from retirement. Has been three days from retirement since 1989.
Diet
Black coffee, regret, and eggs — hard-boiled, like the town he polices.
Partner
Had one. Doesn't talk about it. (It was a spaniel. The spaniel got promoted.)
Vehicle
A 1987 Vauxhall Cavalier the exact colour of leftover mash. The heater only does "sauna".
Method
Turned in his badge and his egg timer at 09:00. Took both back at 09:15. "The case is under my shell now."
Catchphrase
"I'm getting too old for this mash."
▸ Voice-over · Int. Unmarked Cavalier · Night · Rain

The chief told me to let it go. "It's just a potato, Upton." Just a potato. That's what they said about the Great Gravy Ring of '86, and I lost a good spaniel to that one.

See, a case like this doesn't crack open on its own. You crack it. One clean tap on the rim of the truth. Then you peel. And you keep peeling until your eyes water, and everyone at the council offices suddenly remembers a meeting they're late for.

The council says he's being "repaired". Sure. And I'm over easy. Lady, I've been in this game thirty years. I'm hard-boiled — twelve minutes, straight from the fridge, you could bounce me off the pavement and I wouldn't so much as dent.

There's a two-metre spud out there in the dark somewhere, missing his wife, missing an arm — again — and the way I see it, either I find him… or the yolk's on all of us.

Hard-Boiled 101 · A Public Service Glossary

Know Your Hard-Boiled

Following repeated use of the term "hard-boiled" in coverage of this case, readers have asked whether the reference is to detectives or to eggs. Having consulted our legal department, we can confirm it is both. The following glossary is issued as a public service.

Term In An '80s Cop Thriller In The Kitchen
Hard-boiled A cynical detective who's seen too much and feels too little. An egg, 10–12 minutes. Both are impossible to rattle. Both pair badly with soldiers.
Over easy How the council wanted the press to handle this story. Flipped once, yolk still runny. Either way, somebody folds.
Poached What happened to the victim on the night of the van. Lifted gently in barely simmering water. It's still theft, however delicately you do it.
Scrambled The official account of events, upon light questioning. Whisked until nothing holds together. See also: the official account of events.
Cracked The case, wide open, any day now. The shell. The ideal state of a case; the worst state of an alibi.
Sunny side up The BBC's coverage: never flipped, never shows the other side. An egg cooked on one side only. Funny, that.
Shelled up Every witness within a chip's throw of De Bradelei. An egg minding its own business. We respect it. We just need it to talk.
Devilled The scheme, in our considered editorial opinion. Eggs, with paprika. Both look lovely at a buffet. Trust neither.

Case Summary · For New Readers

For readers new to the case: the file, from the top. The coffee is cold. The trail is not.

A Town, A Twin & A Giant Spud

In 2001, Belper's American twin town of Pawtucket — home of Hasbro — gifted us a two-metre Mr Potato Head. He survived twenty-odd years of topplings, lost arms and adventures, and settled at De Bradelei Shopping Village. Belper loved him.

He Vanished. We Panicked.

Earlier this year the statue disappeared from his spot. Posters went up. A website went live. The worst was feared. Edition I: the original appeal →

The "Confession"

Belper Town Council posted on Facebook that they'd "temporarily removed" him for "repair and repainting". We called it what it was. Edition II: the spudnapping →

The Cover-Up

The BBC ran the council's version word for buttery word, then moved on to a story about a dog. Two acronyms, one gravy boat. Edition III: the cover-up →

The Press Conference

Twelve weeks in, with no proof of life, Mrs Potato Head faced the flashbulbs and read the statement reproduced above. As of last night, the case is formally classified as hard-boiled.

Which brings us to tonight. A wife with a very particular set of skills. A detective three days from retirement. A council with no receipts. And somewhere out there, in the rain, a spud. The investigation continues. Readers are advised to keep their eyes peeled.

The Daily Peel is a work of affectionate satire by the Mr Potato Head Belper campaign — a community celebration of a genuinely beloved local statue.
